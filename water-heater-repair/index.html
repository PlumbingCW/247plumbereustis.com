Emergency Water Heater Repair in Eustis, FL

A leaking tank or a cold shower is not a problem to leave overnight. We repair gas, electric and hybrid water heaters in Eustis and tell you honestly when repair no longer makes sense.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

Water heater trouble usually arrives at a bad moment, a cold shower before work or a puddle spreading across the garage floor. We take water heater emergencies on our 24/7 line in Eustis, including nights and weekends. If you see water around the tank, shut off the cold supply valve above it and switch off the power or the gas control until a plumber arrives.

Not every failure means a new tank. A tripped element, a dead thermostat, a failed igniter or a worn relief valve are repairable and often cost far less than replacement. A tank that is leaking from the shell is different. We explain which situation you have, then offer a water heater replacement only when it is the sensible choice.

1

No hot water: what we check first

On a no-hot-water call we work from the simplest cause toward the costly one, and the order is the same for most tank units.

  1. Power or gas supply. A tripped breaker or a closed gas valve explains more cold showers than people expect.
  2. Thermostat and elements. On electric tanks a failed lower element gives lukewarm water and a failed upper one gives none.
  3. Pilot, igniter or flame sensor. On gas tanks a dirty sensor shuts the burner off after a few seconds.
  4. High-limit reset. Overheating trips a safety switch that has to be reset and its cause found.
  5. Tank condition. Heavy sediment or a corroded shell changes repair into a replacement conversation.
2

When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A tank that leaks from the bottom or the seams is nearly always at the end of its life. A leak from a fitting, valve or the relief line can often be fixed. Either way, act quickly:

Cold supply offClose the shutoff on the cold line entering the top of the heater.
Power or gas offFlip the breaker for an electric unit or turn the gas control to off.
Contain the waterGarage and closet units can soak drywall in a few hours, so mop and set out towels.
Do not drain it yourselfHot water scalds. Leave draining to the plumber unless you know the procedure.

Gas, electric and hybrid units fail differently

The type of heater changes the likely repair, which is why the dispatcher asks first.

Heater typeCommon failuresOften repairable?
Gas tankThermocouple, gas control valve, igniter, flue draftYes, unless the tank shell leaks
Electric tankHeating elements, thermostats, high-limit switchYes, parts are low-cost
Hybrid heat pumpCondensate drain, fan, sensors, control boardOften, but parts can be pricier

Gas appliances add a safety note. If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

4

Relief valves, anode rods and the parts people forget

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the safety device that prevents a tank from building dangerous pressure. A valve that drips or discharges regularly is telling you the pressure or temperature is too high, and swapping the valve without finding the cause only hides the warning.

Anode rods sacrifice themselves so the steel tank does not corrode. Checking one every few years can extend a tank, but once the rod is gone and the shell is pitted, repair money is better spent toward a new unit. We will show you the rod condition rather than guessing.

5

Repair or replace: the honest comparison

We use a few simple tests to tell a worthwhile repair from a delay of the inevitable:

  • ✓Age. Most tank heaters give out somewhere between eight and twelve years, so a unit at that age with a major failure is rarely worth a large repair.
  • ✓Where it leaks. A leaking fitting is fixable and a leaking tank is not.
  • ✓Repair cost against the unit's remaining life. A new element is a fair spend on a seven year old tank and a poor one on a fourteen year old tank.
  • ✓Repeat visits. A second failure within a few months usually means more are coming.

7

Water heaters in Florida garages and attics

Many Central Florida homes put the water heater in the garage or in an attic closet, where a leak can run for hours before anyone sees it. If yours is in the attic, a drain pan with a line to the outside is what keeps a slow leak from reaching the ceiling below, and we check it on every visit. Heavy summer humidity also speeds corrosion on exposed fittings.

A surge or outage during hurricane season, which runs June 1 to November 30, can trip a high-limit switch or damage a control board. If your heater fails right after a storm, mention it when you call. We also cover Leesburg and Mount Dora with the same repairs.

8

What the code requires of a water heater

The IPC, which Florida's plumbing code follows, requires a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe on every storage water heater. A valve that drips or a pipe that was capped by a previous installer is a safety defect, not a minor annoyance. We test the valve during every repair visit, and we never plug a discharge line.
