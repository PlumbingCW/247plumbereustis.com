Plumbing Installation in Eustis, FL

Adding a bathroom, upgrading fixtures or moving a sink? We handle plumbing installation for remodels, additions and new construction, with a clear written price before work begins.

Plumbing installation is planned work, and good planning prevents most of the problems we see later. We install fixtures, supply lines, drains, hose bibs and appliance connections in Eustis homes and small commercial spaces. If something is leaking right now, call our 24/7 line and we will handle the emergency first.

Installation jobs range from a single toilet or faucet to a new bathroom or a kitchen remodel. We coordinate with your contractor when there is one, confirm rough-in dimensions and make sure the work meets the local permit requirements.

Planning a remodel: the questions that matter

Moving a toilet or a sink changes the drain, the vent and the supply. A move of a few feet can mean cutting into the slab or reworking the vent through the attic. We tell you early when a layout change creates large cost.

Ask your designer for the fixture schedule, because rough-in dimensions depend on the model. We confirm the numbers before we pipe the wall, which prevents the costly surprise of a fixture that does not fit.

4

Gas lines: a safety-first note

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Do not use switches or phones inside. Gas leaks are a safety emergency, not a plumbing appointment.

For planned gas work, such as a new range or water heater line, the right gas piping, sizing and a pressure test are required. We confirm the scope and permits for your job during booking. Leesburg operates municipal gas service, so addresses there follow a different utility process, as noted on our Leesburg page.

5

New construction and additions

New-build plumbing needs a plan for the slab, the stub-ups and the main sewer connection before the pour. We coordinate with builders on sequencing and make sure the plumbing is inspected before the slab is closed.

For additions, we tie into the existing water and sewer lines and check that the existing capacity can handle the new fixtures. Sometimes an older line needs an upgrade at the same time, and we say so before the plan is final. Owners in newer communities can also read our page on plumbing in Pine Meadows.

8

Why new work needs a permit

New fixtures, new lines and relocated pipes fall under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and the IPC sets trap, vent, spacing and backflow rules for them. Work that is hidden behind a wall is most important to get right the first time, so we plan fixtures and venting before any pipe is cut.
