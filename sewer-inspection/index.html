Sewer Camera Inspection in Eustis, FL

A sewer camera shows what a cable cannot. We run a camera through the line, mark any defect and explain what you are looking at before recommending anything.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A sewer inspection feeds a waterproof camera through your drain or sewer line and records what it sees. It finds the difference between a simple clog and a real defect, and it does so without digging. You watch the monitor, we explain each frame and you leave with a clear picture of the line.

Inspection is a planned service, and it is also the step that makes emergency repairs cheaper. If a clog keeps returning, camera footage tells you whether the next move is hydro jetting or a sewer line repair.

1

What the camera shows

  • ✓Cracks and breaks in clay, cast iron or PVC.
  • ✓Roots entering at joints and how far they extend.
  • ✓Offsets and bellies, where a low spot holds standing water and debris.
  • ✓Scale and grease coating the pipe wall.
  • ✓Collapsed sections and foreign objects.
  • ✓Connections where branch lines tie into the main.

3

Locating the line and measuring depth

Many cameras carry a transmitter that lets us trace the head from above ground. That marks the exact location and depth of a defect, which matters before anyone digs. It also helps when a lateral runs under a driveway, a pool deck or a mature tree.

We can mark the surface with paint or flags and note the footage count so you can find the defect later. If a repair is needed, that location data drives a smaller, more accurate excavation.

4

What an inspection visit looks like

  1. Find access. We use a cleanout, vent stack or removed toilet to reach the line.
  2. Run the camera. The head is fed through the pipe while we record video and distance.
  3. Review together. We pause at each defect and show you what it is.
  4. Locate and mark. A locator tracks the head so the defect can be marked above ground.
  5. Written summary. You get a plain explanation of what we found and which next steps make sense.
5

6

Older homes, lakeside lots and what we look for

Downtown Eustis includes buildings from the 1880s, such as the Iron Block, which shows how long some of the city's buildings have been connected to utility lines. Older neighborhoods may still have original clay or cast iron laterals that are well beyond typical service life. A camera tells you whether the line has held up or needs attention.

Lakeside homes near Lake Eustis and Trout Lake may also have higher groundwater, which can make a cracked line leak inward or outward. If you are weighing a purchase near Mount Dora or Leesburg, the same logic applies.

7

What a camera inspection checks against

A camera inspection is a look at the pipe's condition against what the IPC expects of a drain: continuous slope, sound joints and a clear bore. We record the run, note any bellies, offsets or root entry and explain what we saw. For septic properties, the Florida Department of Health's onsite sewage rules in Chapter 64E-6 of the Florida Administrative Code apply instead.
