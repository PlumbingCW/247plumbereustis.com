How to Hydro Jet a Sewer Line: What Eustis, FL Homeowners Should Know

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water nozzle to scour the inside of a sewer line. It is a professional tool, and a camera comes first.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A hydro jet pushes water through a hose with a special nozzle at pressures far above a garden hose. The jets of water cut away grease, scale, silt and small roots and flush the debris down the line. Unlike a cable, which pokes a hole through a clog, jetting cleans the whole pipe wall.

Homeowners sometimes ask how to do it themselves. The honest answer is that a real sewer jetter is not a consumer tool, and a misused one can damage a pipe or hurt the operator. The sections below explain how a professional does the job, what it clears and what to expect. If you want it done, our hydro jetting service and sewer camera inspection pair naturally.

The short version is this. If water backs up in more than one drain, or a cable clears the line only for a few weeks, ask for a camera look and a jetting quote. If water is rising in a tub or shower right now, stop using water, and call our 24/7 line.

1

How hydro jetting works

  1. A camera looks first. We inspect the line to find the blockage, a crack, a bellied section or a collapsed pipe. Jetting a broken pipe can make things worse.
  2. We open an access point. A cleanout or a pulled toilet provides entry to the line.
  3. The nozzle goes in. A hose with a jet head is fed into the pipe. Forward jets break up the blockage and rear jets pull the hose along.
  4. We work the line in passes. The operator advances and pulls back so the whole length is scoured.
  5. A final camera pass confirms the result. We check that the pipe is clean and note any damage.

3

What jetting cannot fix

Jetting cleans, it does not repair. A cracked pipe, a section that has sagged and holds water or a joint that roots keep entering will clog again after a cleaning. In those cases the camera shows a repair is needed, and our sewer line repair team can explain the options.

Very old clay or brittle pipe is also a risk. High pressure on a weak pipe can break it, which is another reason a camera inspection comes first. A good plumber will tell you if jetting is a bad idea for your line.

4

Eustis note: roots, rain and sewer laterals

Eustis receives about 51 inches of rain a year, mostly from June through September. Wet soil encourages roots to seek the moisture in sewer joints, and lines near mature trees often clog with root fibers. A yearly or biennial look with a camera on a line that has roots is common practice.

Some properties around Eustis, including some in the 32736 ZIP code, may be on septic and not on city sewer. Jetting a septic drain field is not the same job, so tell us what you have. If you are in Mount Dora or Umatilla, the same rules apply. The Eustis Building Department at (352) 483-5462 can answer permit questions about sewer repairs.

5

Signs your line may need jetting

Consider a camera and jetting visit if:

  • ✓Drains throughout the house run slowly or gurgle.
  • ✓A toilet bubbles when the washer drains.
  • ✓Cable cleaning only helps for a few weeks.
  • ✓The line has a history of grease or root problems.
  • ✓A sewage smell comes from a floor drain or yard cleanout.
6

What to do before the plumber arrives for a jetting visit

A few steps make the visit go smoothly:

  • ✓Find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the yard or near the house.
  • ✓Clear the area around it, including pots, mulch and tools.
  • ✓Tell the plumber about past backups, root work and repairs.
  • ✓Stop using water in the house when the plumber asks.
  • ✓Move vehicles so the truck can reach the access point.
7

Keeping the line clear afterward

A clean line stays clean longer when grease stays out of it. Follow the same habits that prevent clogs, such as wiping pans and keeping wipes out of the toilet, and book a camera check on a schedule that fits your line's history.

If roots keep returning, ask about a spot repair or a liner. Jetting every year is less costly than a failed pipe, but a repair can end the cycle.

8

Quick recap on hydro jetting

  • ✓Jetting scours the whole pipe wall, while a cable only bores a hole through a clog.
  • ✓A camera inspection should come first so a damaged pipe is not made worse.
  • ✓It is a professional tool, not a weekend project.
  • ✓Roots can grow back unless a break in the pipe is repaired.
  • ✓Septic systems are different, so tell the plumber what kind of system you have.
9

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires cleanouts at set points in a building drain, so that a line can be cleared without damage. A cleanout makes jetting safer and faster. If you do not have one, ask about adding one, and note that septic properties follow Florida Department of Health rules, Chapter 64E-6 of the Florida Administrative Code.
