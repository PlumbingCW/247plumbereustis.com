Tankless Water Heater Low Pressure in Eustis, FL: Causes and Fixes

Low flow from a tankless heater is usually a clogged screen, scale in the heat exchanger or a partly closed valve.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A tankless water heater will not fire unless enough water flows through it. If flow drops, the unit may deliver lukewarm water, cycle on and off or shut down with an error code. The cause is often simple, such as a clogged inlet screen, and you can check it yourself in minutes.

Work through the checks below from the easiest to the hardest. If you reach scale in the heat exchanger or a failed flow sensor, that is a job for our tankless water heater service team, and if the heater has stopped entirely, our water heater repair line is open 24/7.

1

Check the easy things first

  1. Test other fixtures. If only one tap is weak, the problem is at that fixture and not the heater.
  2. Clean the aerator. Unscrew the faucet aerator and rinse out debris and scale.
  3. Check the isolation valves. Both service valves on the heater should be fully open.
  4. Look at the cold supply. Check that the main valve is fully open and that nothing is restricting the line.
  5. Inspect the inlet screen. With the water off, remove the screen on the cold inlet and rinse it.

3

Scale and flushing

Mineral scale narrows the passages inside the heat exchanger and cuts flow, and it also forces the unit to work harder to heat the water. A descaling flush cleans it out, and it works only if the unit has isolation valves to circulate the solution.

If yours has no valves, a plumber installs them, and that is a good time to set a flush schedule. Skipping this is the most common reason tankless units lose performance within a few years.

4

Eustis note: what we know about local water

Eustis water comes from the Floridan Aquifer, and the city's 2023 report lists chlorine averaging 1.2 ppm but no hardness figure. Without a published number, the practical step is to ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 or test your tap. If you see white crust at your faucets, scale is likely building inside the heater too.

If you are on a private well outside the city system, there is no public report, so a lab test is essential before choosing a flush schedule or a treatment system.

5

When low flow means a bigger issue

See a plumber if you notice:

  • ✓An error code that returns after you reset the unit.
  • ✓A burning or gas smell, in which case leave and call the gas utility or 911.
  • ✓Water that is cold even with full flow.
  • ✓A heater that clicks and shuts off repeatedly.
  • ✓Noise from the unit, such as banging or rumbling.
6

Low flow or a long wait for hot water?

Two different complaints get described as low pressure. One is weak flow at the fixture, which points to a restriction. The other is a long wait for hot water, which is about the distance from the heater to the tap and has nothing to do with pressure.

A recirculation pump or a small point-of-use tank can shorten the wait, and a clogged screen or scale will not. Knowing which problem you have saves a service call, so time how long it takes hot water to arrive and compare.

7

The cold water sandwich

If your hot water turns cold for a few seconds and then warms up again, you may have what owners call a cold water sandwich. It happens when you turn the tap off and on quickly, and the unit shuts down and restarts. Using a slightly higher flow or a different fixture can help.

If the effect is severe, a buffer tank or a recirculation setup can smooth it out. A plumber can tell you whether the unit, the settings or the layout is the cause.

Quick recap for a weak tankless heater

  • ✓Check the aerator, the isolation valves and the inlet screen first.
  • ✓Separate weak flow from a long wait for hot water, since they have different fixes.
  • ✓Scale is a common cause, and a flush with isolation valves clears it.
  • ✓Write down any error code before you reset the unit.
  • ✓A gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility or 911.
11

What the plumbing code says

Tankless units rely on a minimum flow rate set by the manufacturer, which is why low flow can shut one down. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) also caps static pressure near 80 psi, so very high pressure is not the problem. If flow is low, check the screen, the valves and scale, and follow the manufacturer's flush instructions.
