How to Repair a Leaky Toilet Tank in Eustis, FL

A leaking toilet tank can come from five places. A dye test and a dry paper towel show which one in about ten minutes.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

Before you buy parts, find where the water is coming from. A toilet tank can leak at the supply connection, at the tank bolts, at the tank-to-bowl seal, through a crack in the porcelain or simply by sweating in humid air. Each has a different fix, and guessing wastes both money and towels.

In a humid place like Eustis, condensation is a frequent false alarm. A cold tank in a warm, damp bathroom collects water droplets that drip onto the floor, and no part is broken. The steps below separate sweating from real leaks, and if you find a crack or the leak keeps coming back, our toilet repair service can help.

1

Run the ten-minute test

  1. Dry everything. Wipe the tank, the supply line and the floor with a dry towel.
  2. Add dye. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and let it sit for twenty minutes without flushing.
  3. Check the bowl. Colored water in the bowl means a leaking flapper, which is a silent leak and not the one on your floor.
  4. Use dry paper towels. Press a paper towel against each joint, the supply connection, the bolt heads and the tank base, and see which one gets wet.
  5. Watch for sweat. If the whole outside of the tank is beaded with water, it is condensation, not a leak.
2

Where the leak is and how to fix it

Where water appearsCauseRepair
Under the tank at the boltsWorn rubber washers on the tank boltsReplace bolts and washers, tighten evenly
Between the tank and bowlFailed tank-to-bowl gasketReplace the gasket and reseat the tank
At the supply line connectionLoose nut or worn washerTighten by hand plus a quarter turn or replace the line
A crack in the porcelainImpact or ageReplace the tank or toilet, because cracks cannot be repaired reliably
All over the tank in humid weatherCondensationInsulating liner, a tempering valve or better bathroom ventilation
3

Fixing a bolt or gasket leak

Turn off the supply, flush to empty the tank and sponge out the remainder. Disconnect the supply line and remove the nuts under the bowl, then lift the tank off. Replace the bolts, washers and the large rubber gasket, and set the tank back with even pressure on both bolts.

Tighten the nuts a little at a time, alternating sides, because porcelain cracks under uneven force. A wobbling tank or an overtightened one both leak. If the porcelain has hairline cracks around the bolt holes, the tank itself is failing and replacement is the safer choice.

4

Eustis note: humid summers and tank sweat

Eustis has a humid subtropical climate with highs in the lower 90s in summer and about 51 inches of rain a year, mostly from June through September. That humidity is why tank condensation is a regular complaint in Central Florida homes, especially in bathrooms without a working exhaust fan.

If you want to rule out a plumbing leak behind the wall, our leak detection service uses moisture meters and pressure tests. The Eustis Building Department at (352) 483-5462 handles questions about permits if you plan a larger remodel.

5

When replacing the toilet is smarter

A repair is not always the best use of money. Consider replacement if:

  • ✓The tank or bowl has a crack, even a hairline one.
  • ✓The toilet is more than twenty years old and clogs often.
  • ✓The glaze is badly stained or worn.
  • ✓You have had two or more repairs in a year.
  • ✓You want a more efficient model and a taller bowl.
6

Why a flapper leak matters even when the floor is dry

A tank that leaks into the bowl never touches your floor, so many people do not notice it. The fill valve opens every few minutes to refill the tank, and the water runs down the drain unseen. Over a month this can add a surprising amount to your water bill.

To check, listen for the tank refilling when nobody has flushed. If you hear it, the flapper or its seat is worn, or the chain is too tight. Replacing a flapper takes minutes, costs little and is worth doing at the first sign of ghost flushing.

7

Tools and parts checklist

Gather these before you start:

  • ✓A new tank-to-bowl gasket and a bolt kit if you will lift the tank.
  • ✓A new flapper that fits your flush valve size.
  • ✓A braided supply line, sized for your shutoff.
  • ✓An adjustable wrench, a screwdriver and a sponge.
  • ✓A bucket, old towels and food coloring for the leak test.

9

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires toilets to be set on a sound flange and sealed to the drain, and federal rules limit new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush. A leak at the tank bolts or the base usually means a sealing part has failed. Replacing it restores the seal the code expects.
