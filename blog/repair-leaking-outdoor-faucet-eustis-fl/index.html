How to Repair a Leaking Outdoor Faucet in Eustis, FL

An outdoor faucet leaks from the spout, the handle or the pipe behind the wall. The fix depends on where the water shows up.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A dripping spout on a hose bib usually means a worn washer inside the faucet. Water leaking around the handle points to the packing nut, and water coming through the wall or the siding points to a leak behind it. The first two are small repairs, and the third is one to take seriously, because a leak inside a wall can rot framing and feed mold.

Start by looking at where the water appears. Then turn off the water to the faucet and use the steps below. If you see damp siding, a stain on an interior wall near the faucet or water that returns after a repair, call our team for a leak detection visit.

2

Repairing a leaky hose bib step by step

Most hose bibs can be repaired in under half an hour.

  1. Shut off the water. Close the shutoff valve that feeds the faucet or the main house valve.
  2. Remove the handle. Take out the screw and slide the handle off.
  3. Loosen the packing nut. Use a wrench, then unscrew the stem.
  4. Replace the washer or stem. Match the old part, or take it to the store to find the right one.
  5. Reassemble and test. Tighten the packing nut just enough to stop a leak at the handle, then turn the water on and check.
3

Vacuum breakers and backflow protection

Many outdoor faucets carry a vacuum breaker, a small cap that keeps hose water from flowing back into the house supply. If it fails, it can spray from the top of the faucet and, more importantly, stop protecting your drinking water from a hose left in a bucket or a pool. Replacement is simple and cheap, and it is worth doing.

If your faucet is old and has no breaker, a new one with a built-in breaker is a better replacement than a patch. We install them as part of a faucet repair visit.

4

Eustis note: heat, rain and hose bibs

Eustis sees summer highs in the lower 90s and about 51 inches of rain a year. Heat and sun harden rubber washers on exposed hose bibs faster than most people expect, so a drip after a few seasons is typical. Winter lows in the mid-40s mean freeze damage is uncommon in a normal year, though hard freezes do occur in Central Florida, as the Great Freeze of 1894 and 1895 reminded Eustis growers.

On cold nights, covering an exposed faucet and disconnecting hoses is a small precaution. If you are unsure whether your faucet needs a permit to move or replace, the Eustis Building Department at (352) 483-5462 can answer.

5

When a leak inside the wall is the real problem

These signs mean the leak is not just at the faucet:

  • ✓A dark stain on the interior wall behind the faucet.
  • ✓Soft or discolored siding below the faucet.
  • ✓Water coming back after a good repair.
  • ✓A water meter that keeps spinning with everything off.
  • ✓A musty smell in the room behind the faucet.
6

Choosing a replacement faucet

A standard hose bib is inexpensive and fits most walls. A model with a built-in vacuum breaker adds backflow protection, which keeps hose water from being siphoned back into your supply. Pick brass over plastic for any faucet that sits in the sun, because brass handles heat and years of use better.

Check the thread type and the length of the shank before you buy. A faucet that is too short will not seat against the wall, and one that is too long will bump into the siding. When in doubt, bring the old faucet to the store or send us a photo.

7

Prepare the faucet for storm season

Summer storms bring wind and debris that can knock a hose over and yank a faucet. Disconnect hoses when you leave for a trip, and do not leave a spray nozzle under pressure. A hose left connected under pressure puts constant strain on the faucet and its seals.

8

Quick recap for the faucet

  • ✓A drip from the spout usually means a worn washer.
  • ✓Water at the handle points to the packing nut.
  • ✓Wet siding or an interior stain means a leak inside the wall and needs a plumber.
  • ✓Replace the vacuum breaker if it sprays, because it protects your drinking water.
  • ✓Add a shutoff inside if there is none, so the next repair is easier.
9

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires hose connections to have backflow protection, and many outdoor faucets use a vacuum breaker built to ASSE 1011 for that purpose. If yours has none, add one when you repair the faucet. It keeps hose water, which can contact fertilizer, pool water or bucket contents, from being drawn back into the house supply.
