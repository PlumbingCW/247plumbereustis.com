How to Repair a Shower Faucet Diverter in Eustis, FL

If water keeps pouring from the tub spout when you want the shower, the diverter is worn, stuck or dirty. Most are repairable.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A diverter is the part that sends water either to the tub spout or up to the showerhead. On a three-handle shower it is a separate valve. On a single-handle shower it may be built into the main cartridge, and on a tub spout it can be a small pull-up knob. When it fails, the symptoms are easy to spot: weak shower pressure, water dribbling from the spout while the shower runs, or a knob that will not stay up.

Matching the type of diverter to the fix saves time. A spout diverter is a quick swap that most homeowners can do, while a diverter inside the wall needs the handle and trim removed and, sometimes, a plumber. If you are unsure what you have, our faucet repair team can identify it quickly. The sections below walk through each type.

1

Identify which diverter you have

TypeHow to recognize itCommon failure
Tub spout diverterA small knob or ring on top of the tub spout that you pull upWorn internal gasket, so water leaks from the spout while the shower runs
Three-handle valveA separate center handle that controls where water goesWorn seat or stem washer
Two-handle with a diverterA lever or knob between the hot and cold handlesScale or a worn seal
Single-handle cartridgeOne handle with a built-in diverterThe cartridge wears out and must be replaced
2

Fixing a tub spout diverter

This is the easiest repair and the most common one.

  1. Check how the spout attaches. Look for a small set screw under the spout, or see whether it simply screws on.
  2. Remove the spout. Loosen the set screw with a hex key, or turn the spout counterclockwise.
  3. Clean the connection. Wipe off mineral deposits and old sealant.
  4. Install a matching new spout. Take the old one to a store to match the length and thread.
  5. Seal and test. Wrap the threads or apply sealant as directed, then run the shower and check that the water goes up.
3

Repairing a diverter inside the wall

For a three-handle or cartridge diverter, begin by shutting off water to the bathroom and removing the handle and trim plate. The stem or cartridge is behind them. Take it out, clean the seat, replace the washer or the cartridge and rebuild in reverse order.

The tricky part is getting the right replacement part. Cartridges look alike across brands and differ in small ways, and a wrong one will leak or bind. If there is no access panel behind the wall, or the valve body is corroded, a plumber is the safer choice because a mistake can turn a small repair into a wall repair.

Eustis note: heat, minerals and wear

Eustis water comes from wells in the Floridan Aquifer, and the city's 2023 report lists chlorine at an average of 1.2 ppm but gives no hardness figure. Without a published number, ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 or test your tap before assuming scale is the cause. White crust on a showerhead is a warning sign for small parts like a diverter gasket too.

If your shower is in a rental or a condo around Leesburg or Mount Dora, check with the property manager before opening the wall. For permit questions on a larger remodel in Eustis, the Building Department at (352) 483-5462 can answer.

5

When a diverter repair is not the real fix

Call a plumber if you notice:

  • ✓Water pressure is low at the showerhead even with the diverter fully up.
  • ✓Hot and cold temperatures swing without warning, which can mean a failing cartridge.
  • ✓Water leaks inside the wall around the handle or in a ceiling below.
  • ✓The handle spins freely and does nothing.
  • ✓The valve body itself is cracked or badly corroded.
6

Parts and tools you need

A diverter job needs only a few items:

  • ✓A hex key set for set screws on spouts.
  • ✓A screwdriver and an adjustable wrench.
  • ✓Plumber's tape or the sealant the spout maker recommends.
  • ✓A replacement spout, stem or cartridge matched to your brand.
  • ✓A towel to cover the drain so that small parts cannot fall in.
7

Preventing the next failure

Diverters wear faster when they are forced shut or left half open. Push the knob up fully and let it drop back on its own, and do not hang a bag or a washcloth on it. A shower filter or a regular rinse of the showerhead keeps minerals from building up around the seal.

If you rent in Leesburg or live in Mount Dora, tell your landlord or manager about the leak early. A small failure is an easy fix and a long-running leak is not.

8

Quick recap for the shower

  • ✓Spout diverters are the easiest to fix and the most common to fail.
  • ✓Wall diverters and cartridges need the handle and trim removed.
  • ✓Match the replacement part to your brand and model.
  • ✓Low pressure, temperature swings and wall leaks mean a bigger problem than the diverter.
  • ✓Call a plumber if you cannot reach the valve from behind or the body is corroded.
9

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires shower valves to be pressure-balancing or thermostatic types listed to ASSE 1016, so that flushing a toilet does not scald you. If you replace a cartridge, use one made for your valve body. A wrong cartridge can break that protection, which is another reason to match parts carefully.
