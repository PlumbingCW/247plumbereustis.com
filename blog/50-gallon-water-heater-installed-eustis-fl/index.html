50 Gallon Water Heater Installed in Eustis, FL: What the Job Includes

A 50 gallon tank suits most Eustis households of three to five people. The install is more than swapping the tank, and the extras are what protect you.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A 50 gallon water heater is the most common size for a family home. It holds enough hot water for back-to-back showers and a dishwasher cycle, and it fits the same closet or garage corner that older 40 and 50 gallon tanks used. If your tank is leaking, rusted at the base or more than a decade old, replacing it with another 50 gallon unit is often the simplest path.

The price you are quoted depends on what the installer includes. A good installation covers the tank, a new expansion tank where required, a safe drain pan, a pressure relief valve and discharge pipe, fresh supply connectors, a gas or electric connection that meets the manufacturer's instructions, and removal of the old tank. Our water heater replacement visits cover all of these, and the sections below explain each one so you can compare quotes fairly.

1

What a complete 50 gallon installation includes

Ask any installer whether the quote covers these items:

  • ✓A new 50 gallon tank sized and rated for your fuel type, gas or electric.
  • ✓New flexible water connectors and shutoff valves on the hot and cold lines.
  • ✓A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe run to a safe spot.
  • ✓A thermal expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system.
  • ✓A drain pan with a drain line where a leak could damage a floor or a finished space.
  • ✓For gas units, a sediment trap, a flexible gas connector, a leak test and a vent that draws properly.
  • ✓Haul-away of the old tank and a check of the area for water damage.
2

Gas or electric: what changes in the install

ItemGas 50 gallonElectric 50 gallon
Fuel connectionGas line, shutoff, sediment trap and flexible connectorDedicated circuit with the correct breaker and wire size
VentingFlue or sealed vent that must pull properlyNone required
Recovery speedFaster, so the tank refills hot water soonerSlower, so heavy use can run the tank down
Safety checksCombustion and leak testing, carbon monoxide awarenessWiring, breaker and element checks
Swap difficultyHigher if the vent or gas line needs changesHigher if the circuit needs upgrading

Switching fuel types is possible, but it is a larger project because a new gas line or a new electric circuit is needed.

3

Is 50 gallons the right size for your home?

Tank size is matched to how many people use hot water at the same time. As a general guide, a 40 gallon tank suits two or three people and a 50 gallon tank suits three to five. Larger households, a big soaking tub or heavy laundry habits may push you to a larger tank or a high-recovery model.

A larger tank also costs more to keep hot. If you are a couple in a small house, a 50 gallon tank may be more than you need, and a smaller unit lowers the standby heat loss. If you often run out of hot water on a 50 gallon tank, we can tell you whether a bigger tank, a faster-recovery unit or a switch to a tankless unit makes more sense.

4

Eustis note: water, permits and what to ask the city

The City of Eustis supplies water from wells in the Floridan Aquifer and publishes an annual report. In the 2023 report, chlorine averaged 1.2 ppm, and no hardness figure is listed. Because mineral scale builds on the bottom of a tank and on heating elements, ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 about your water or test your tap. If you see white crust at faucets, plan to flush the tank each year.

The Eustis Building Department at (352) 483-5462 states that plumbing, gas and mechanical work need a permit and asks owners to call before beginning. Water heater replacement usually falls under that, so confirm the process for your address. If you live in nearby Mount Dora or Leesburg, the permit office for your town is different, and we handle that process on the jobs we perform.

5

How the installation day goes

  1. We shut off the water and the fuel. Gas valve or breaker first, then the cold supply, and the old tank is drained.
  2. The old tank is disconnected and removed. We cut off worn connectors and carry the tank out.
  3. The area is checked. We look for rust, water stains and rot under the old unit and set the drain pan.
  4. The new tank is set and connected. Water lines, relief valve, expansion tank and fuel connection go in per the instructions.
  5. We fill, test and light or power the unit. We check for leaks and combustion and confirm the water reaches temperature safely.
6

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Florida's plumbing code follows, requires a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, a pan where leaks could cause damage and an expansion tank on closed systems. For gas units, the Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas, governs the gas line. A quote that leaves these out is not a complete installation.

7

What to do if your tank is leaking right now

Act in this order while you wait for a plumber:

  • ✓Turn off the gas valve or the breaker for the water heater.
  • ✓Close the cold water shutoff on top of the tank.
  • ✓Do not touch the unit if water is near electrical parts.
  • ✓If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911.
  • ✓Open a hot tap and drain the tank to the floor drain or outside if it is safe.
8

What moves the price of a 50 gallon install

Two houses with the same tank can have different bills.

What moves the price
  • $Fuel type and whether the vent or gas line must change.
  • $Tank brand, warranty length and efficiency rating.
  • $The location, such as an attic, a closet or a garage.
  • $Code upgrades like an expansion tank, a pan or a corrected discharge pipe.
  • $Permit costs and after-hours work if the tank has failed overnight.

You hear the price before work begins. If the tank is leaking, see water heater repair first, since some failures can be fixed without a new unit.

50 gallon water heater questions

Many tanks last roughly eight to twelve years, depending on water quality and maintenance. A tank that is leaking from the body cannot be repaired and should be replaced.

Technically, some people do. Gas connections, venting and relief valves have safety consequences, and a permit is required, so most homeowners hire a licensed plumber.

A straightforward swap usually takes a few hours. Venting changes, a new circuit or a tight attic can extend the visit.

If the tank is sound and not leaking, a new anode rod can extend its life. Once the tank body leaks, replacement is the only fix.
