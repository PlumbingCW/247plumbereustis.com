Plumbing a Tankless Water Heater in Eustis, FL: What the Install Involves

A tankless heater is not a drop-in swap for a tank. The plumbing, gas, venting and power around it often change.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A tankless water heater heats water as you use it, so the plumbing around it must deliver high flow and, on a gas model, plenty of fuel. That is why a switch from a tank can include a larger gas line, new venting, an upgraded electrical connection and a condensate drain. The heater is the small part of the quote.

Planning the installation before you buy a unit avoids surprises, and our tankless water heater service page covers repair and flushing once it is installed. The sections below list what each part of the system needs.

The short version is this. A tankless unit is a good fit when the gas line, vent and power can support it at a fair price, and a new tank is the better choice when they cannot. A site visit settles it, and the price you hear is the price before work begins.

2

Gas line sizing and why it matters

A tankless unit can pull several times the gas of a tank burner when it fires at full power. If the line is too small, the heater may not reach full output, shut down on a fault or supply lukewarm water when several fixtures run. A plumber calculates the demand of every gas appliance and checks the line against it.

In some homes the existing line is adequate. In others a new, larger run from the meter to the heater is required. This is the most common reason a tankless quote comes in higher than expected, and we tell you before you commit.

3

Venting and combustion air

Venting is a safety issue, not a style choice. We check:

  • ✓The manufacturer's approved material and maximum vent length.
  • ✓Clearances from windows, doors and other openings.
  • ✓Whether the unit pulls combustion air from inside or outside.
  • ✓Condensate handling on condensing models.
  • ✓Whether the existing flue can be reused. Often it cannot.

Eustis note: water quality, the permit office and your utility

The City of Eustis supplies water from the Floridan Aquifer, and its public report lists chlorine and fluoride results and lead and copper sampling but no hardness figure. Because scale shortens a tankless unit's life, ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 about your water or test your tap before settling on a flush schedule.

The Eustis Building Department at (352) 483-5462 says plumbing, gas and mechanical work needs a permit and asks owners to call before beginning. A tankless install involves all three, so confirm the process for your address.

5

Tank or tankless: an honest comparison

  • ✓Tankless gives continuous hot water and frees floor space, but costs more to install and needs regular flushing.
  • ✓A new tank is faster and cheaper to install and is simple to service, but it can run out and takes more room.
  • ✓Hybrid heat pump tanks use less electricity and need ventilation and a condensate line.
  • ✓Your fuel may decide the matter. Gas tankless is common, while electric tankless needs a large circuit.
6

Questions to ask a plumber before you sign

Compare quotes on these points:

  • ✓Which gas line size and length did you calculate, and what is the total load?
  • ✓What vent material and route will you use?
  • ✓Are isolation valves and a flush kit included?
  • ✓Is a permit included, and who handles the inspection?
  • ✓What does the manufacturer's warranty require, such as registration or annual service?
  • ✓What happens to the old tank, and is haul-away included?
7

Keeping a tankless unit healthy after the install

A tankless unit needs a descaling flush on a schedule that matches your water, and the inlet screen should be checked from time to time. Skipping maintenance is the most common reason owners lose efficiency within a few years.

Keep the area around the heater clear, and never store paint or cleaners near a gas unit. If you see an error code, write it down before you reset it. Our tankless service team can read what the code means.

Quick recap before you buy a tankless unit

  • ✓The gas line, vent, power and water lines all need to be checked.
  • ✓Gas line size is the most common surprise in the quote.
  • ✓Ask about isolation valves, flushing and the permit.
  • ✓Know your water, because scale shortens the life of the heat exchanger.
  • ✓Compare a new tank, a hybrid and a tankless unit on the full installed price.
10

What the plumbing code says

Three rule sets govern a tankless install. The Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas volume, which is based on the International Fuel Gas Code, covers gas pipe sizing and venting. The manufacturer's instructions set vent materials and lengths. The National Electrical Code (NEC) covers the electrical supply. A permit ties them together.
