How to Keep Drains From Clogging in Eustis, FL Homes

Most clogs build up over months. A few habits in the kitchen, the bathroom and at the toilet stop most of them.

By the Eustis 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

Drains clog because something sticky or solid builds up on the pipe wall until water can no longer pass. In the kitchen that something is grease and food. In bathrooms it is hair and soap, and at the toilet it is wipes and too much paper. Small habits in each place prevent most of the calls we answer.

No habit protects a pipe that is already damaged. If the same line keeps clogging, a camera shows whether the cause is a crack, roots or a low spot, and our drain cleaning team can find out. The tips below cover the three main zones in a home.

The short version is this. Keep grease, wipes and hair out of the pipes, clean stoppers and traps on a schedule, and have a camera look at any line that has clogged more than once.

1

Kitchen: keep grease and food out

  • ✓Pour grease into a can, not down the drain. Let it cool, then throw it away.
  • ✓Wipe pans and plates with a paper towel before washing.
  • ✓Use a sink strainer to catch food scraps and rice.
  • ✓Run cold water for 30 seconds before, during and after using the disposal.
  • ✓Skip coffee grounds, pasta and eggshell membranes in the disposal.
2

Bathroom: catch hair and soap

A cheap hair catcher in the tub and shower drain stops most of the hair that ends up wound around the trap arm. Clean it every week or two. Bar soap leaves a waxy film, and heavy use of body washes with oils also coats the pipe, so flushing with hot water from time to time helps.

In a sink, toothpaste, cosmetic residue and hair all collect in the trap. If a sink drains slowly, remove the stopper and clean it before assuming the pipe is blocked. A slow drain that stays slow after cleaning may need a cable.

3

Toilet: only paper and waste

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper, and they are a leading cause of main line clogs. Paper towels, feminine products and cotton swabs have the same problem. Putting them in the trash prevents most toilet and lateral clogs.

Low-flow toilets also have less force to push waste, so a clog is more likely when too much paper is used at once. If your toilet clogs repeatedly, the line behind it may be partly blocked, and a camera check can confirm it.

4

A simple maintenance schedule

How oftenWhat to doWhy
WeeklyClean the tub and shower hair catcherPrevents hair buildup in the trap
MonthlyFlush kitchen and bath drains with hot, not boiling, waterHelps move soap and grease along
Every few monthsClean sink stoppers and aeratorsRemoves residue before it hardens
Every year or twoHave a camera look at older or root-prone linesFinds damage before a backup

Skip chemical drain openers. They rarely clear a solid blockage, and they can damage older pipes and make the line unsafe to work on.

5

Eustis note: rain, roots and septic

Eustis gets about 51 inches of rain a year, mostly from June through September, and saturated soil can push groundwater and roots against older lines. If drains slow only after heavy storms, tell your plumber so the lateral is checked, not just the fixture.

Some properties outside the city limits, such as in the 32736 ZIP code, may be on septic systems, and septic rules differ from sewer. Ask the City of Eustis utilities office or the Lake County health department which system serves your home, and tell your plumber. A septic tank that is full backs up no matter how careful you are with the sink.

6

Teach the whole household

Most clogs come from habits that one person does not know about. A child may flush wipes, a roommate may rinse bacon grease and a guest may use the disposal for rice. A small sign near the sink and a short talk with everyone in the house go a long way.

Keep a lidded can under the sink for grease and a small bin in each bathroom so that wipes, cotton swabs and dental floss have somewhere to go besides the toilet.

7

Checks before storm season

Summer rain is heaviest from June through September, so late spring is a good time to look at your drains. Clean stoppers, flush traps with hot water and walk the yard to look at the cleanout cap. A buried or cracked cap lets water and soil into the line.

If the main line has clogged before, a camera check before storm season shows whether roots or a sag are building again.

9

Quick recap of the habits that matter

  • ✓Grease goes in a can, never down the sink.
  • ✓Hair catchers in tubs and showers save the most calls.
  • ✓Only paper and waste go down the toilet.
  • ✓Clean stoppers and traps every few months.
  • ✓Book a camera check if a line has clogged more than once.
10

What the plumbing code says

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires every trap to hold a water seal and every drain to be vented, and a trap or vent that is partly blocked slows a drain even when the pipe is clear. If cleaning a stopper does not help, the vent may be at fault, and a plumber can check it.

11

When to call a plumber

  1. One drain is slow. Clean the stopper and trap first, then call if it stays slow.
  2. Two fixtures are slow together. Call. A branch line is likely partly blocked.
  3. Every drain is slow or gurgling. Stop using water and call. The main line needs attention, see sewer line cleaning.
  4. The same clog returns. Book a camera or jetting visit before the next backup.
  5. Sewage is rising. Treat it as an emergency and call our 24/7 line.
