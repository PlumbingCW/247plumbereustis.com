What older Mount Dora houses ask of their plumbing

A house built before the middle of the twentieth century may have galvanized supply lines that have narrowed inside, cast iron or clay drains that have cracked, and layers of later repairs in different materials. Pressure drops, rusty first-draw water and drains that slow after a few years are all common in that housing stock.

When a camera shows a failing lateral, we compare spot repair against replacement, as covered on the sewer line repair page. When galvanized lines fail repeatedly, repiping is the lasting fix. We tell you which applies after we have looked, not before.

Restaurants, inns and shops downtown

Downtown Mount Dora is built around restaurants, inns and boutiques, so grease and heavy-use restrooms drive most business calls. A kitchen line that backs up during service is costly, and a grease interceptor that is overdue for pumping is a common cause. Our commercial plumbing service handles after-hours calls like these.

If your business sits in a historic building, check with the city before altering visible plumbing. Our crews work around opening hours when the problem allows it.

5

Lakeside homes and groundwater

Homes near Lake Dora, Lake Gertrude and Lake Beauclair sit closer to the water table, and a cracked line can leak inward and send groundwater into a sewer or outward into the soil. Water that surfaces in a yard far from any sprinkler is a signal to run a trace, and our leak detection service uses acoustic and pressure methods before anyone digs.

Irrigation lines are another source of lake-side leaks. A zone that runs weak or a patch that stays wet in dry weather is worth checking before the next bill arrives.

Plumbing code and permits in Mount Dora

We tell you which permit office to call for your address.
