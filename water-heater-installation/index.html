Water Heater Installation in Eustis, FL

Planning a new water heater, moving one or upgrading to a larger tank? We handle the sizing, the gas or electrical side and the clean installation, with a clear price before work starts.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A planned installation gives you time to pick the right heater instead of whatever is in stock during a failure. We install tank, hybrid and gas water heaters in Eustis homes, and we help you size the unit for how your household actually uses hot water. If your current tank has already failed, see water heater replacement for the same-day route.

Good installation is mostly the parts nobody photographs: a pan and drain line where a leak would damage a floor, an expansion tank where the system needs one, a vent that draws properly and shutoffs you can find in the dark. Those details are what separate a heater that lasts from one that causes a second visit.

1

Sizing a water heater for your household

Tank size matters less than how the water gets used. A family that runs two showers and a dishwasher together needs a different recovery rate than a couple that showers separately. Gas tanks generally recover faster than electric tanks of the same size, which is why a smaller gas unit often matches a larger electric one.

We ask how many bathrooms you have, whether you run a soaking tub or a dual-head shower, and what time of day demand peaks. A fifty gallon tank is a common choice for a typical family home, and we explain when going larger or smaller is smarter.

What a careful installation includes

These are the parts of a job we put in the written quote:

  • ✓A new temperature and pressure relief valve with a correctly run discharge line.
  • ✓New flexible water connectors and accessible shutoff valves.
  • ✓A drain pan with a line to a safe termination wherever a leak could reach finished space.
  • ✓An expansion tank on closed systems where the plumbing needs one.
  • ✓Venting inspected and corrected on gas units, with a combustion check before we leave.
  • ✓Hauling away the old heater and packing materials.
4

Installing a heater in an attic, closet or garage

Location changes the work. A garage install is the easiest to access and the easiest to protect with a pan. An attic or interior closet needs a drain line that actually reaches the outdoors, because a slow leak over a finished ceiling is expensive to find.

If you want to move the heater, plan on new supply lines and possibly new venting or electrical work. We walk the proposed spot with you and flag problems before you commit, such as clearance, floor load or a flue that cannot run the distance.

5

From call to finished install

  1. Tell us about the home. Share the old heater's size and fuel, plus where it sits and how you use hot water.
  2. Site check and quote. We confirm sizing, venting and utilities, then give a clear written price.
  3. Schedule the install. Same-day appointments are available when the calendar allows, and planned dates are easy to book.
  4. Install and test. We set the unit, connect it, vent it, fill and test it, and check for leaks.
  5. Walk-through. You see the shutoffs, the relief valve and the drain pan, and we answer questions about maintenance.

7

Permits and Florida licensing

In Eustis, the Building Department at (352) 483-5462 says a permit is required for plumbing, gas, electrical and mechanical work, so ask whether your project needs one before work begins. Outside city limits the rules may differ, and we confirm the right office for your address.

Hire only a plumber you can verify. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, at myfloridalicense.com, lets homeowners look up a contractor by name or license number. We work in Mount Dora, Leesburg and the rest of the area with the same standard.

8

Codes that shape a new installation

A new installation follows the manufacturer's instructions and the codes behind them, namely the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and for gas units the Fuel Gas volume, which is based on the International Fuel Gas Code. Venting, clearances, relief piping and the gas shutoff are all inspected items, so a permit is part of the plan.
