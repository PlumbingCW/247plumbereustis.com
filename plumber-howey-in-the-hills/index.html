Howey-in-the-Hills is a small town of about 1,643 residents on the west shore of Little Lake Harris. William J. Howey platted it in 1921 and nicknamed the rolling terrain the Florida Alps, and the town was renamed Howey-in-the-Hills in 1927. Many homes here are newer and larger than the typical in-town house, and the plumbing needs show it.

We dispatch from Eustis, about ten miles north along SR 19, and the line is open 24/7. Larger homes bring bigger water heaters, longer lines and landscape irrigation, and each of those has its own failure modes.

1

Large homes, big demand: water heaters and recovery

Houses with several bathrooms and soaking tubs can overwhelm a single tank during a busy morning. When a heater fails in a large home, replacement is a chance to rethink size or move to a tankless or hybrid unit. Our water heater installation page explains the options and the extra gas or electrical work some require.

If your heater is leaking now, close the cold supply valve and cut the power or gas first. We will book the next available plumber and discuss replacement or repair on site.

3

Newer homes, older surprises

Newer does not mean trouble-free. These are the problems we meet in recent builds:

  • ✓Pressure spikes from a failing regulator that stress fixtures and supply lines.
  • ✓Builder-grade fittings that fail early, such as plastic supply lines and flappers.
  • ✓Irrigation leaks from broken heads or a cracked line in a large yard.
  • ✓Long hot-water waits in homes with far-apart bathrooms, solved with a recirculation loop.
  • ✓Scale buildup in tankless and tank heaters, discussed under water treatment.
4

Lake Harris, groundwater and leak detection

Properties near Little Lake Harris sit close to the water table, and a wet patch in the yard may be groundwater, irrigation or a supply line. Water that surfaces in dry weather, or a meter that keeps turning with the house off, is a reason to book leak detection. Tracing the leak first avoids digging in the wrong place across a big lot.

The Town of Howey-in-the-Hills Public Works and Utility Department provides water service in town. For a main break at the street or a town-side water question, contact the town, and call us for anything inside your property line.

5

Hills, SR 19 and how we reach you

SR 19, known locally as Palm Avenue, runs through town and leads about eight miles north to Tavares and about 13 miles south to Groveland. Yalaha, to the northwest, shares the 34797 ZIP code that we also serve from this dispatch point. Clermont lies further south, and we mention it for homeowners who work or shop there and ask whether we can reach them.

Plumbing code and permits in Howey-in-the-Hills

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, applies statewide, and each town runs its own permit office. In Howey-in-the-Hills we work to the same International Plumbing Code (IPC) rules that apply in Eustis. Properties on septic follow the Florida Department of Health onsite sewage rules, so tell us which system serves your home.
