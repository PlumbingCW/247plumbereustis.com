Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Eustis, FL

A disposal that hums but will not spin, leaks under the sink or backs the kitchen up needs prompt help. We repair or replace the unit and quote first.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A dead garbage disposal can stop a kitchen. A humming unit that will not spin, a unit that trips its breaker or a leak under the sink floods the cabinet and the floor. Turn off the power at the wall switch and the breaker before touching anything, then call our 24/7 line.

A disposal that jams is often fixable in minutes. A leaking housing is not, and a unit that backs up into the dishwasher may be a drain problem rather than a disposal problem. We sort out which before you spend money, and we also install new disposals with correct connections.

Humming, jammed or leaking: what to do now

Never put a hand in a disposal. These steps keep it safe.

Power offTurn off the wall switch and the breaker before anything else.
Do not use the unitRunning a jammed unit burns out the motor.
Stop the sinkIf water is leaking, close the sink shutoff valves and set a bowl under it.
Call and describeTell us the sound, the leak location and whether the sink drains.

Replacing a disposal: what to look for

When replacement is the answer, these are the choices that matter:

  • ✓Horsepower. A half or three-quarter horsepower unit suits most homes, and larger households may prefer more.
  • ✓Mounting system. Matching the existing sink mount saves time.
  • ✓Noise insulation is worth it in open-plan kitchens.
  • ✓Continuous or batch feed. Batch feed adds a safety measure for homes with children.
  • ✓Dishwasher connection and a clean knockout plug are checked at installation.
4

Installation steps

  1. Remove the old unit. We disconnect power, the drain and the mounting assembly.
  2. Prepare the sink. The flange is cleaned and reseated with fresh putty.
  3. Mount and connect. The new disposal is mounted, the drain and dishwasher line are connected and the electrical is checked.
  4. Test. We run water and the unit to confirm it spins freely and nothing leaks.
  5. Advise. We explain what should and should not go down the disposal.
5

What should not go in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, eggshell membranes, celery strings, pasta and rice cause more repeat clogs than any hardware failure. A disposal grinds food into small pieces, but the pipe beyond it still has to carry them, and grease coats the walls and narrows the line.

Run cold water before, during and after use for about half a minute so the waste moves into the larger drain. If your kitchen line is slow even with careful habits, a camera or a jetting visit may be the right next step.

8

Disposal wiring and drains

A disposal draws power under the National Electrical Code (NEC) and discharges through a trap built to the IPC. A loose cord, a missing receptacle or a drain without a trap breaks those rules. We check both sides of the connection, and we recommend a licensed electrician if a new circuit is required.
