Tankless Water Heater Service in Eustis, FL

Install, repair, flush and descale for on-demand water heaters. We diagnose low flow, error codes and lukewarm water, and we tell you when a service visit is all the unit needs.

☎ Call (321) 463-9969

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it never runs out the way a tank can. It also has less tolerance for neglect. Scale in the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen or a blocked vent shows up as low flow, error codes or hot water that fades in the middle of a shower.

We service tankless units in Eustis as scheduled work and as urgent repairs. If yours has stopped heating altogether, our 24/7 line can send someone, and the first call is often a water heater repair diagnosis. If you are weighing a switch from a tank, we cover that below.

1

Flushing and descaling: the service most units are missing

Scale builds inside the heat exchanger wherever water is heated, and it lowers efficiency and flow long before the unit fails. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the exchanger using isolation valves and a small pump, then rinses the system clear. Many manufacturers call for this on a regular schedule, and the right interval depends on your water.

Because Eustis's public report does not list a hardness figure, the practical step is to ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 or test your tap, then set the flush schedule from what you learn. Units installed without isolation valves need them added before a proper flush can happen, which we install during the visit.

3

Hot water that fades or runs cold in the middle

The brief cold slug between two hot bursts is a known behavior of on-demand heaters, caused by a short shutoff and restart. Low flow fixtures, a blocked inlet screen or a mixing valve in a shower can make it worse. A recirculation line, a buffer tank or a flow adjustment often fixes it.

If the problem appeared suddenly rather than gradually, we look at scale and the inlet first. Gradual decline over a year or two usually points to the heat exchanger, which is where a flush pays for itself.

4

Installing tankless in an existing home

Switching from a tank is a larger project than swapping one tank for another. We check these before quoting:

  • ✓Gas line capacity. Tankless units pull more gas at peak than a tank burner and often need a larger line.
  • ✓Venting. Many models need stainless or polypropylene venting rather than the old flue.
  • ✓Electrical. Gas models need power for the fan and controls, and electric tankless units need a large dedicated circuit.
  • ✓Condensate drain. High-efficiency units produce acidic condensate that needs a neutralizer or proper drain.
  • ✓Wall space and clearances for the unit and service valves.
5

Tankless service steps

  1. Describe the symptoms. Tell us the brand, the code if any, and when the problem shows up.
  2. Inspect and test. We check supply, venting, inlet screen and flow, and read the stored error history.
  3. Quote first. You get a clear price for a flush, a part or a larger repair before work starts.
  4. Service and verify. We flush or repair, then run hot water at several fixtures to confirm stable temperature.
  5. Set a plan. We note when the next flush should happen and keep the isolation valves accessible.

8

Gas, vent and electrical rules for tankless units

Tankless installs touch three rule sets. The Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas, which is based on the International Fuel Gas Code, sets gas pipe sizing. The manufacturer sets vent materials and lengths, which the code requires installers to follow. The National Electrical Code (NEC) covers the power supply. We check all three before we quote, because they decide whether your home can take the unit.
