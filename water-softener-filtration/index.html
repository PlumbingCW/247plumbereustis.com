Water Softener and Filtration in Eustis, FL

White crust, spotted glass, chlorine taste or an odor from the tap all point to a water quality question. We test first, then recommend only the equipment your water needs.

Water treatment is a planned project, and the right system starts with knowing what is in your water. We test your tap and explain the results before suggesting a softener, a carbon filter or reverse osmosis. If a failed water heater or a leak is your immediate problem, call our 24/7 line first and come back to treatment later.

The City of Eustis publishes an annual water quality report, and its latest report lists a chlorine disinfectant averaging 1.2 ppm and a copper result well under the action level. It does not list a hardness figure, so we do not guess at yours. A test of your own tap is the honest starting point.

1

What the city report tells you and what it does not

Eustis draws its drinking water from wells in the Floridan Aquifer, according to the city's report. The report covers regulated substances, the disinfectant level and lead and copper sampling. For hardness and the specifics at your faucet, ask the Eustis Water Department at (352) 357-5618 or test the tap.

Homes outside the city system, including some near Umatilla and Howey-in-the-Hills, may use private wells. A private well has no public report at all, so a lab test is essential before choosing equipment.

3

Softeners, filters and reverse osmosis

  • ✓Softeners swap hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, protecting heaters, faucets and fixtures from scale.
  • ✓Carbon filters reduce chlorine taste and odor and some organic compounds.
  • ✓Reverse osmosis polishes drinking water at a single tap.
  • ✓Sediment filters protect fixtures and downstream equipment from particles.
  • ✓Whole-house systems treat water at the main, while point-of-use units treat only one tap.
4

Why treatment can protect your plumbing

Scale lowers the efficiency of a water heater and shortens the life of a tankless unit's heat exchanger. It can also clog aerators and show up as dripping faucets. If you have a tankless heater, see our tankless service page for flush intervals.

A softener also changes how soap behaves and how fixtures spot. It will not fix every problem, and we are honest about that. Taste and odor issues are often better solved by a carbon filter than by a softener.

7

Treatment standards worth knowing

Water treatment equipment is certified to published standards. NSF/ANSI 44 covers cation exchange softeners, NSF/ANSI 53 covers filters that reduce health-related contaminants and NSF/ANSI 58 covers reverse osmosis systems. Check a unit's listing before you buy. The IPC also requires backflow protection and an air gap at the drain connection.
